THE CENTRAL FINDING

A shorter survey retained most ranking performance.

On validation data, eight-input gradient boosting achieved AUROC 0.821, compared with 0.828 for all 21 inputs. The final cutoff is 0.40.

01 · THE DATA

Adult survey research

253,680 US adult records from the 2015 CDC survey dataset via UCI. Stratified 60/20/20 training, validation, and test split; fixed seed 42.

02 · THE TRADEOFF

Detection has a cost

The model detects more positive labels than the partial quiz, with fewer false-positive flags. Its low precision means many flagged records carry negative labels. Those flags are not proven unnecessary tests.

03 · THE LIMITATION

Age performance varies

Validation recall was 52.2% for ages 18–44 and 92.2% for ages 65+. Independent evaluation is needed before considering clinical use.

Comparison scope: The partial CDC approximation omits family history and gestational diabetes. Self-reported diagnosis labels do not establish performance in undiagnosed people. A lower score cannot rule out diabetes or prediabetes.

How it was built

Small model, checked honestly.

Data253,680

US adults in the CDC's 2015 health survey (BRFSS), via the UCI repository.

Model76 trees

Gradient boosting on 8 of the survey's 21 questions. It runs inside this page.

Cut-off0.40

Picked on validation data before the test set was opened, to catch most cases.

Ranking quality0.82

AUROC on the test set (1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is a coin flip). The partial CDC approximation scored 0.76; this is not overall accuracy.

What it can't do

  • Diagnose anything. Only a blood test can.
  • Rule diabetes out. On the test set it missed 13% of cases.
  • Know your family history. These variables are absent from this dataset. Every CDC comparison here omits family history and gestational diabetes.
  • Speak for everyone. The answers are self-reported, from US adults in 2015.

Where your answers go

  • Answers and predictions stay in the current browser tab. The model and styling are included in this file; no third-party fonts or prediction service are loaded.
  • The "about X in 100" count describes reported outcomes in a validation score band. It is not an estimate validated for your personal clinical risk.
  • Data: CDC Diabetes Health Indicators (UCI).
